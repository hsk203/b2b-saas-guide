If you run a small marketing agency, you live in automation tools. Client onboarding, lead routing, reporting, social posting, CRM updates — the work that eats your team's hours is exactly what Make.com and Zapier exist to eliminate. But the two tools charge in completely different ways, and at agency volume the price gap is enormous.

This comparison uses each tool's official pricing as of October–November 2026, plus hands-on reviews. Prices change; verify on make.com/pricing and zapier.com/pricing before you buy.

## The short answer

- **Pick Zapier** if you want the easiest setup, the biggest app catalogue (9,000+ apps), and you run only a few simple automations. A non-technical team can be live in minutes.
- **Pick Make** if you run multi-step workflows at volume, need visual branching and error handling, and want a bill that does not explode as you scale. At 10,000 monthly events, Make is roughly 7× cheaper.

## How they charge: credits vs tasks

This is the single most important difference, and most comparison articles get it wrong.

| | Make.com | Zapier |
|---|---|---|
| **Billing unit** | Credit — every module that runs, including the trigger check | Task — only a successful action step; the trigger is free |
| **Free plan** | 1,000 credits/mo, multi-step scenarios, 2 active scenarios | 100 tasks/mo, 5 Zaps, 2-step workflows only |
| **Entry paid plan (annual)** | Core ~$9/mo, 10,000 credits | Professional ~$20/mo, 750 tasks |
| **Mid tier (annual)** | Pro ~$16/mo, 10,000 credits | Professional 2,000 tasks ~$49/mo |
| **App catalogue** | 3,000+ apps | 9,000+ apps |
| **AI features** | Make AI, or bring your own key | Copilot builds Zaps from plain language |

**The Make caveat nobody updated:** on 19 January 2026, Make renamed "operations" to "credits", and a polling trigger now burns 1 credit on every check even when it finds nothing. A 1-minute polling scenario eats 43,200 credits a month — more than 4× the Core allocation. Use webhooks (instant triggers) instead of polling wherever possible.

**The Zapier advantage:** triggers, filters, and built-in tools (Formatter, Filter, Paths, Delay, Looping) are all free. You only pay when an action step successfully runs.

## Real costs: three agency scenarios

### Scenario 1: Solo freelancer, 5 client workflows

- **Zapier:** Professional 1,500-task tier ≈ $29/mo.
- **Make:** Core $9/mo with massive headroom.
- **Winner:** Make, unless you need an app only Zapier has.

### Scenario 2: Small agency, 5-step lead-routing workflow at 2,000 runs/month

This is the scenario that decides it for most agencies. A 5-step workflow (trigger + 4 actions):

- **Make:** 2,000 runs × 5 modules = 10,000 credits — exactly the Core base at ~$9/mo.
- **Zapier:** 2,000 runs × 4 actions (trigger excluded) = 8,000 tasks — far past the 750-task Professional tier, landing around $60–80+/mo once you size up.
- **Winner:** Make, by a wide margin.

### Scenario 3: 10,000 events/month

- **Make:** ~$10.59/mo (Core, monthly billing).
- **Zapier:** ~$73.50/mo (Professional 2,000-task tier).
- **Winner:** Make is roughly 7× cheaper. But note the counting models differ — Zapier's free triggers and filters can offset some of the gap on filter-heavy workflows.

## Where Zapier wins

- **Easiest on-ramp.** Zapier is live in 3 minutes. Copilot builds a Zap from a plain sentence. A non-technical team member can ship an automation this afternoon with no training.
- **Biggest app catalogue.** 9,000+ apps covers enterprise ERP and niche tools Make does not have.
- **Compliance.** SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are in place, which matters if your clients ask.
- **Reliability.** Zapier's infrastructure is battle-tested at enormous scale.

## Where Make wins

- **Price at volume.** The gap widens as you scale. At agency volume, the difference is hundreds of dollars a month.
- **Visual depth.** The visual builder shows the whole workflow as a diagram, with routers, filters, iterators, and error handlers visible at a glance. Complex logic is far easier to build and debug.
- **Free plan is genuinely useful.** Multi-step scenarios on the free plan (Zapier's free plan is 2-step only).
- **Data operations.** Make handles arrays, iterators, aggregators, and data transformation natively — work that needs extra steps (and extra tasks) on Zapier.

## Which one should your agency pick?

- **3–5 simple automations, non-technical team, low volume:** Zapier. The premium is worth it for the zero learning curve.
- **Multi-step workflows, branching logic, 10+ automations, or anything over ~1,000 runs/month:** Make. The price gap is too large to ignore.
- **Already on one of them and it works:** stay. Migration cost (rebuilding every workflow, re-testing, re-training the team) usually exceeds a year of price difference.

## The n8n option

If your agency has technical staff, n8n deserves a look: $20/mo billed annually for 2,500 executions, or free if you self-host. One execution is a full workflow run (not per-step), which makes it cheaper than both at high volume. The trade-off is that you maintain the infrastructure yourself.
